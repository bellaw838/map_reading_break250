# Reading Analysis Marking Guide

Use this as a map of the reading moves an advanced reader makes. The goal is not to memorize terms. The goal is to know what question each mark helps you answer.

---

## The Big Picture

Almost every reading-analysis question asks one of five things:

| Big question | Marks that help |
|---|---|
| **What is this text really saying?** | `T`, `MI`, `TH`, `CL` |
| **How do I prove it?** | `E`, `RSN`, `TRAP` |
| **What is suggested but not directly said?** | `INF`, `POV`, `SPK` |
| **How does the author feel or make me feel?** | `TO`, `M`, `WC`, `CON` |
| **How is the text built?** | `STR`, `PF`, `SHIFT`, `FIG` |

If you get lost, come back to these five questions.

---

## Family 1 — What Is The Text Really Saying?

These marks are about the point of the text.

| Mark | Term | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| `T` | **Topic** | What the text is about in 1-3 words. Example: friendship, climate change, school rules. |
| `MI` | **Main Idea / Central Idea** | What an informational text says about its topic. Usually a full sentence. |
| `TH` | **Theme** | The deeper message in a story or poem. Also a full sentence, not one word. |
| `CL` | **Claim** | What the author is trying to prove, especially in argument writing. |

### Easy Confusion

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| **Topic vs Main Idea** | Topic = subject. Main idea = what the author says about that subject. |
| **Main Idea vs Theme** | Main idea is usually for informational text. Theme is usually for literature and poetry. Both answer: "What is the text really saying?" |
| **Main Idea vs Claim** | In argument writing, the main idea is often the author's claim. |

---

## Family 2 — How Do I Prove It?

These marks are about proof.

| Mark | Term | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| `E` | **Evidence** | The exact words or details that prove an answer. |
| `RSN` | **Reasoning** | How the evidence supports the claim. |
| `TRAP` | **Trap Answer** | Why a wrong answer is tempting but not proven. |

### Easy Confusion

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| **Evidence vs Inference** | Evidence = what the text says. Inference = what you conclude from the evidence. |
| **Evidence vs Reasoning** | Evidence is the proof. Reasoning explains how the proof supports the answer. |
| **True Detail vs Best Evidence** | A detail can be true but still not be the strongest proof for the question. |

---

## Family 3 — What Is Suggested But Not Said?

These marks are about clues, voice, and point of view.

| Mark | Term | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| `INF` | **Inference** | What the text suggests but does not directly say. Formula: clue + reasoning. |
| `POV` | **Point of View / Narrator** | Who is speaking? Can we trust them? What do they know or not know? |
| `SPK` | **Speaker vs Author / Poet** | Especially in poems: the "I" in the poem is not always the poet. |

### Easy Confusion

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| **Inference vs Guess** | An inference must be supported by a clue in the text. A guess is not proven. |
| **Author vs Narrator / Speaker** | Author = real writer. Narrator or speaker = voice inside the story or poem. |
| **Stated Fact vs Inference** | If the text directly says it, it is not an inference. |

---

## Family 4 — How Does The Text Feel?

These marks are about attitude, emotion, and word choice.

| Mark | Term | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| `TO` | **Tone** | The author's or speaker's attitude. Example: skeptical, admiring, frustrated, neutral. |
| `M` | **Mood** | The feeling created in the reader. Example: tense, peaceful, gloomy, hopeful. |
| `WC` | **Word Choice / Diction** | Important words the author chose and what they imply. |
| `CON` | **Connotation** | Positive, negative, or neutral feeling of a word. |
| `P` | **Author's Purpose** | Why the author wrote it: inform, persuade, entertain, explain, warn, criticize, celebrate. |

### Easy Confusion

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| **Tone vs Mood** | Tone = author/speaker's attitude. Mood = feeling created in the reader. |
| **Word Choice vs Connotation** | Word choice is the selected word. Connotation is the feeling that word carries. |
| **Word Choice vs Tone** | Word choice is often the evidence for tone. |
| **Purpose vs Main Idea** | Purpose = why the author wrote it. Main idea = what the author says. |

---

## Family 5 — How Is The Text Built?

These marks are about structure, function, and literary technique.

| Mark | Term | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| `STR` | **Text Structure** | How the text is built: cause/effect, compare/contrast, sequence, problem/solution, description. |
| `PF` | **Paragraph Function** | What job a paragraph does: introduce, explain, give evidence, contrast, conclude. |
| `SHIFT` | **Shift** | A change in tone, idea, argument, or direction. |
| `FIG` | **Figurative Language** | Metaphor, simile, personification, imagery, symbolism, hyperbole. |
| `CTR` | **Counterclaim / Rebuttal** | Opposing view + how the author answers it. |

### Easy Confusion

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| **Structure vs Paragraph Function** | Structure = whole-text pattern. Paragraph function = one paragraph's job. |
| **Structure vs Shift** | Structure is the overall design. Shift is the moment the design changes direction. |
| **Figurative Language vs Theme** | In poems and stories, metaphors and images often carry the theme, but they are figurative language first. |
| **Claim vs Counterclaim** | Claim = author's position. Counterclaim = opposing position. |

## How To Use This On A Passage

1. Read once for understanding — no marks.
2. Read again and ask the five big questions.
3. Mark only the words that help answer those questions.
4. After reading, write three sentences:
   - Main idea or theme
   - Tone, with one word-choice clue
   - Strongest evidence

Do not mark everything. Good marking is selective.

---

## Most Important For MAP 250+

For MAP Reading 250+, the highest-leverage marks are:

- **Word Choice**
- **Evidence**
- **Inference**
- **Tone**
- **Main Idea / Theme**
- **Author's Purpose**
- **Text Structure**

The key habit: do not just choose the answer that sounds smart. Choose the answer the text proves best.
