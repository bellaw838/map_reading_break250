### 10. A — A specific sentence quoted directly from the passage

### 11. C — It happens within an argument or debate

- **Exact support:** “He **conceded** that his original **argument** was flawed but stood by his overall conclusion.”
- **Second-best:** A. *Conceded* and *admitted* overlap, but they are not identical; *conceded* often suggests yielding a point after resistance or within a disagreement.
- **Verdict:** **Revise**
- **Why revise:** C is the best available answer, but it does not capture the most precise distinction. The stronger teaching point is that *conceded* suggests reluctantly acknowledging a point against one’s position. Also, “original argument” already tells the reader that the exchange is argumentative, so C partly repeats context rather than testing the verb’s nuance.

### 13. C — The cleanliness is odd enough that it should be noticed

### 14. A — Point to the specific words in the text

- **14:** “Prove” is too absolute for textual evidence; use “support.”

- **24:** “Nostalgia” appears without any memory or past-time cue.

- **14:** The item’s wording says evidence should “prove” a claim, when “support” is more accurate.
