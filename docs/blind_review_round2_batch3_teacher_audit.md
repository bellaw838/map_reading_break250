### 10. (b2/03-proof-vs-related)

**Answer: A — Ask which one proves the specific claim, not just which is on topic.**

## Separate flags

### Two answers are equally or substantially defensible

- **Question 21:** A and B both reasonably explain why a true detail attracts students on a main-idea question. The stem needs to describe a specific student error rather than ask for a broad causal explanation.

### Unclear wording or unfair distractors

- **Question 10:** “Proves” is too absolute; use “directly supports.”
- **Question 13:** The passage gives a before-and-after association, while the claim uses causal wording.
- **Question 21:** Two plausible explanations compete.
- **Question 35:** “Too weak to defend” is less precise than “unsupported.”
- **Question 41:** “Proves” is again too absolute.
- **Question 45:** A full sentence that makes a claim is not automatically a theme.

### Intended answer claims more than the passage proves

- **Question 13:** Attendance rose after the policy, but the passage does not establish that the policy caused the rise.
- **Questions 10 and 41:** The general rule that evidence “proves” a claim is stronger than is normally justified for interpretive reading questions.

### Narrator, speaker, and author mix-ups

- **None identified in this batch.**
- Question 14 appropriately asks about the narrator’s tone and provides evaluative narrator wording.
- Questions 30 and 46 also provide enough framing to judge authorial or speaker attitude.

### Answers that depend on one word rather than context

- **No unfair single-word dependency identified.**
- Questions 22, 34, 37, 38, and 39 are deliberately vocabulary or connotation questions, and their surrounding context supports the answers.
- Questions 30 and 46 use signal words, but the larger contrast and sentence framing—not one isolated word—establish the tone.

## Overall judgment

This is a strong batch. Forty of the 46 items have one clearly best answer. The five revision items mostly need wording changes rather than replacement, and only Question 21 is invalid for single-answer scoring in its current form.
