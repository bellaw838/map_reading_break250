**Exact support:** In academic use, *refute* means successfully showing that a claim is false, normally through reasoning or evidence.

### 12. Evidence that new turbines reduce noise

**Exact support:** The passage states exactly that “**New ‘silent’ turbine designs use curved blades that reduce noise by 40 percent**.”

### 13. Inference about the boy and the menu

**Answer: B — The boy may not have money for food.**

**Exact support:** He “**hadn’t eaten since breakfast**,” “**stared at the menu for a long time**,” and then ordered only “**a glass of water**.” That combination can suggest that he wanted food but may have been unable to buy it.

**Second-best: A — The boy had not eaten since breakfast.** This is directly stated and therefore is not an inference.

**Verdict: Revise.**

**Reason:** B is plausible, but the clues do not point uniquely to lack of money. He could be indecisive, fasting, waiting for someone, feeling ill, or choosing not to eat. Because “may not” sets a very low standard, almost any possible explanation could count as supported.

**Recommended fix:** Add a money-related clue, such as “he checked the prices twice” or “counted the coins in his pocket,” or change the stem to “Which answer is an inference rather than a stated fact?”

### 15. Word strength

**Answer: A — *suggests* and *proves***

**Exact support:** *Suggests* presents a possible or supported conclusion; *proves* claims certainty. Replacing one with the other can substantially change the strength of a reading answer.

**Second-best: C — *large* and *very big*.** These differ somewhat in style and emphasis, but not nearly as much in certainty or evidential force.

**Verdict: Clear.**

### 44. Best move with imagery

**Answer: A — Ask what feeling or meaning the detail is building.**

**Exact support:** The question concerns what an advanced reader should do with “**vivid sensory detail**.” Interpreting its effect moves beyond merely identifying or visualizing it.

**Second-best: B — Picture the scene as exactly as the words describe it.** Visualizing is a valid first step and may be necessary before interpretation, which makes it a reasonably strong distractor.

### 45. Certainty signaled by *implies*

**Answer: A — “implies”**

**Exact support:** “**implies**” indicates that the evidence points toward a conclusion without directly establishing it. The surrounding phrases “**may be flawed**” and “**stop short of proving it**” reinforce the limited certainty.

**Second-best: B — “flawed.”** This describes the possible problem with the methods, but it does not itself indicate how certain the conclusion is.

**Verdict: Revise.**

**Reason:** The unlisted word “**may**” is at least as clear—and arguably clearer—as a signal that the conclusion is not certain. The item asks “Which word” rather than “Which of these listed words,” so a careful reader may reasonably identify *may*.

**Recommended fix:** Change the stem to: “What does the word ‘implies’ signal about the certainty of the conclusion?” Or ask, “Which listed word signals that the conclusion is not certain?”

## Separate validity flags

### 1. Two answers are equally or substantially defensible

- **Question 41:** D is the richer inference, but A also adds the defensible inference “that evening.” The stem does not ask for the strongest or most complete inference.
- **Question 44:** A is the intended advanced-analysis move, but B is also a legitimate immediate reading move. The sequence should be stated.
- **Question 5:** The missing article prevents genuine comparison against the source passage.

### 2. Unclear wording or unfair distractors

- **Question 5:** “Same article as above” has no usable referent in this packet.
- **Question 13:** The money inference is only one of several plausible explanations.
- **Question 34:** “Argument” is too narrow for all nonfiction.
- **Question 44:** “Best mental move” is subjective without saying “after picturing the detail.”
- **Question 45:** The most obvious uncertainty word, “may,” appears in the sentence but not among the options.

### 3. Answer claims more than the passage proves

- **Question 13:** The passage does not specifically establish financial difficulty.
- **Question 46:** The passage does not state what the policy was intended to accomplish or whether complaints measure its success.
- **Question 41:** D is well supported, but “colder” is still inferred from one action and should be presented as the best combined inference rather than the only inference.

### 4. Narrator, speaker, and author confusion

- **No definite narrator/speaker/author mix-up found in this batch.**
- **Question 32** is acceptable because both the narrator’s phrase “brushed off” and the official’s quoted response support *dismissive*.
- **Question 38** explicitly asks about the narrator’s word choice and uses genuinely evaluative narration.

### 5. Answers depending on one word rather than context

- **Question 45 is the problematic case.** It explicitly targets “implies,” while the surrounding sentence contains the even clearer uncertainty signal “may.”
- **Questions 11, 22, 27, and 28** are direct vocabulary items by design. They are acceptable because the task is explicitly to define or distinguish the target word; Questions 22 and 27 also provide contextual support.
- **Question 36** does not depend only on “supposedly”: the later criticism and inaccessibility evidence reinforce the skeptical framing.
- **Question 43** uses the signal phrase “By contrast,” but the paragraph’s actual content also performs a contrast.

## Highest-priority fixes

1. **Question 5:** Supply the missing article or rewrite the stem so the item stands alone.
2. **Question 41:** Remove the inferred phrase “that evening” from A, or ask for the inference that combines both clues.
3. **Question 46:** Establish the policy’s intended goal before asking whether it may not be working.
4. **Question 45:** Target *implies* directly or acknowledge that *may* is also an uncertainty signal.
5. **Question 13:** Add a price or money clue if financial difficulty is the intended inference.
6. **Question 44:** Make the sequence “picture first, interpret next” explicit.
7. **Question 34:** Replace “argument” with “text’s explanation or argument.”
