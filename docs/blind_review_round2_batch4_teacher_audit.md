### 1. c2/01-word-precision

- **Answer: B**
- **Exact support:** “cars and farms remain the largest sources” shows that the factory is only one contributor among several.
- **Second-best: A.** “Contributes” may sound less accusatory than “causes,” but politeness is not the main semantic difference. The sentence is carefully limiting the factory’s share of responsibility.
- **Verdict: Clear**

### 2. c3/01-academic-verbs

- **Answer: D**

- **Second-best: C.** A journalist might later summarize a report, but “scrutinized” does not mean summarized.
- **Verdict: Clear**

### 3. a1/01-theme-message-not-topic

### 4. b1/02-main-idea-vs-detail

### 5. a4/03-mood-vs-tone

### 7. b4/01-neutral-vs-skeptical

- **Answer: A**
- **Exact support:** “what it called” distances the author from the startup’s label “revolutionary,” and “though” introduces evidence that challenges that label.
- **Second-best: D.** “Nearly identical” and “last year” also undermine the startup’s claim, so they are meaningful evidence of skepticism rather than a weak distractor.

- **Concern:** A is the best answer, but D is also substantially defensible because the comparison itself supports the skeptical reading.
- **Recommended fix:** Change the stem to: **“Which pair most directly signals the author’s skeptical framing?”** Then A is clearly best because it identifies the distancing and contrast markers rather than the underlying facts.

## Separate flag review

### Items where two answers are equally or substantially defensible

- **None fully equal.**
- **Question 7** is the closest case: A is the better answer, but D also provides meaningful evidence of skepticism.
- **Question 33** crosses structural analysis and meaning, though A remains the better first tool.

### Unclear wording or unfair distractors

- **7:** A and D both support skepticism; the stem should ask for the most direct framing signals.
- **15:** “Most common” is an unsupported ranking, and “prove” is too absolute.
- **19:** The definition of tone is too narrow.
- **28:** The keyed wording makes an unsupported causal claim.
- **33:** The stem overlaps two reading tools.
- **46:** The answer assumes an article position not supplied in the excerpt.

### Answers or stems that claim more than the passage proves

- **15:** “Prove the claim” should usually be “directly support the claim.”
- **28:** “The ban produced modest gains” should be “The ban was followed by modest gains.”
- **46:** “Counterargument to the article’s view” cannot be established without the article’s surrounding position.

### Narrator / speaker / author mix-ups

- **19:** Tone is defined only as the narrator’s attitude. The correct general definition must allow for an author, narrator, or speaker.

### Problematic dependence on one word rather than context

- **None.** The vocabulary-focused items intentionally test words such as “scrutinized,” “weathered,” “undermines,” “meticulous,” “sanguine,” “ambiguous,” and “ignited,” but each supplies enough surrounding context or a precise conceptual contrast. The connotation items also use the whole sentence rather than assuming that a word has one fixed emotional value.
